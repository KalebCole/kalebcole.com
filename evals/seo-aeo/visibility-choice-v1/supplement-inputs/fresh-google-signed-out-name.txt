Background and Activities
Cole joined the Atomwaffen Division in 2016 and quickly rose to a leadership position within the Washington state chapter. Under his direction, the group stockpiled firearms, conducted paramilitary "hate camps" in the forests of Western Washington, and actively prepared for what they envisioned as a catastrophic race war. He gained international scrutiny when he was deported and banned from Canada due to his extremist affiliations, and photos later surfaced of him posing with the Atomwaffen flag outside the Auschwitz concentration camp in Poland.

Criminal Plot and Sentencing
In early 2020, federal authorities arrested Cole alongside three co-conspirators for orchestrating "Operation Erste Säule". The multi-state conspiracy explicitly targeted journalists, media investigators, and anti-hate advocates—specifically focusing on Jewish individuals and journalists of color. The group mailed and glued highly intimidating posters to the victims' homes, which depicted figures in skull masks carrying Molotov cocktails toward burning houses, accompanied by text warning that "actions have consequences".

While his co-conspirators chose to plead guilty, Cole proceeded to a federal jury trial in Seattle, where he was convicted on all five felony counts in September 2021. On January 11, 2022, U.S. District Judge John C. Coughenour sentenced Cole to 84 months (7 years) in federal prison. Cole filed an appeal against his sentencing enhancements, but the U.S. Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed his conviction and original sentence, ruling that the propaganda campaign constituted true threats.
