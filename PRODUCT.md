# Product

## Register

**Brand.** This is a personal publication where the design, writing, and
visible human authorship are the product. It is not a product dashboard,
professional portfolio, or conversion surface.

## Users and Audience Promise

The site is for curious people who value Kaleb's perspective. Builders and
technical peers are the current core audience, but they are not a permanent
gate on who the site serves or what it may discuss.

Visitors should be able to meet a real person, understand what currently
matters to him, and find something worth reading, saving, or following. They
can expect first-person context, honest uncertainty, visible enthusiasm, and a
clear distinction between Kaleb's own thinking and work he points to from
elsewhere.

## Purpose and Success

This is Kaleb's personal publication: a public workshop for ideas,
experiences, interests, projects, and recommendations while they are alive and
evolving. It exists to make writing and sharing the default, not to operate as
a marketing funnel, polished resume, or claim of finished authority.

A successful visit leaves someone with a clear sense of Kaleb and at least one
idea, story, or recommendation that mattered to them. Success is demonstrated
by meaningful reading, return visits, saves, follows, and direct human
connection rather than conversion theater or inflated engagement metrics.

## Voice and Personality

The governing personality is **candid, curious, and enthusiastic**.

Preserve the voice already present in Kaleb's writing: personal, direct,
willing to say "I think," open about what remains uncertain or unsuccessful,
and excited without manufacturing certainty. Editing should clarify that
voice, not replace it with institutional polish, generic expertise, or
detached brand copy.

Interface language is plain and conversational. It names the thing, gives the
reader enough context to act, and stops. Humor and informal phrasing are
welcome when they sound like Kaleb rather than a copywriting persona.

## Content and Publishing Boundaries

Kaleb is the editorial boundary. The publication may follow any topic he
chooses without forcing every contribution into a professional, technical, or
personal-brand theme.

- **Writing** develops Kaleb's own idea, experience, or perspective.
- **Recommends** points to someone else's work or to something Kaleb values. A
  recommendation may include a concise optional annotation called **My
  thoughts**.
- **Projects** are things Kaleb made or is exploring, presented plainly with
  useful context rather than inflated case studies, achievement metrics, or
  portfolio theater.

These are publishing modes, not topic restrictions. A contribution belongs
where its editorial intent is clearest. Writing and Recommends remain distinct
feeds so readers can tell authorship from curation.

Publication should preserve exact author wording and links unless a change is
explicit, reviewable, and accepted. Writing may openly identify itself as
unfinished when that state is honest and intentional; repository content
marked as a draft remains unpublished.

## Anti-references

- A polished personal-brand resume built from achievement metrics, showcase
  projects, client-style case studies, conversion calls to action, and
  "thought leader" copy.
- Generic AI-polished minimalism: tasteful but interchangeable design and copy
  that could belong to anyone, erases uncertainty, or makes Kaleb sound more
  authoritative than he is claiming to be.
- A content dashboard that gives every publishing mode equal homepage weight
  and makes the visitor choose between competing feeds before meeting Kaleb.
- Any treatment that turns visible human authorship into a professional
  persona or makes the writing secondary to self-promotion.

## Strategic Principles

1. **Meet a person, not a pitch.** Let visitors understand Kaleb before asking
   them to evaluate credentials, projects, or professional value.
2. **Preserve the fingerprints.** Clarify rough or enthusiastic expression
   without sanding away first-person language, uncertainty, humor, or changing
   interests.
3. **Show the current truth.** Prefer an honest view of what Kaleb thinks and
   explores now over a timeless but artificial claim of expertise.
4. **Let the subject move.** Organize contributions by editorial intent, not by
   a permanent topical niche.
5. **Keep writing central.** The homepage introduces Kaleb and moves directly
   into recent writing. A compact recent-recommendations sibling may follow
   without competing for the opening hierarchy.
6. **Curate without ranking people.** Recommends is one chronology that readers
   may filter by medium. It has no priority subtype or editorial importance
   tier.
7. **Keep access ahead of effect.** Personality, imagery, and motion must never
   make the publication harder to read, navigate, or understand.

## Accessibility and Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the minimum standard, not a stretch goal. Every public surface
must support keyboard-only and screen-reader use, visible focus, sufficient
contrast, text zoom and responsive reflow, understandable structure and link
language, descriptive alternatives for meaningful media, and experiences that
do not depend on color alone.

Motion must respect reduced-motion preferences, and no essential content or
interaction may depend on animation, hover, timing, or gesture. Both visual
modes must preserve the same hierarchy and comprehension. Accessibility is a
constraint on every future product and design decision rather than a cleanup
pass after implementation.

## Page and Product Scope

The redesign specification governs:

- **Shared publication shell:** identity, primary navigation, theme choice,
  feed discovery, footer, metadata, error states, and quality gates across
  every public route.
- **Homepage:** a candid introduction and portrait followed immediately by
  recent writing, then a deliberately capped compact view of recent
  recommendations. Projects do not receive a homepage section.
- **Writing index:** the newest published writing receives priority, followed
  by one reverse-chronological archive.
- **Writing article:** a quiet long-form reading experience with clear
  authorship, useful metadata, media and code support, a personal sign-off, and
  simple onward navigation.
- **Recommends:** one newest-first list, optional My thoughts annotations,
  medium filters, real source artwork when available, plain empty states, and
  its own RSS feed. Topic tags may remain in metadata and feed categories but
  are not visible in recommendation rows.
- **Projects:** a valid publishing mode and navigation destination, but not a
  homepage section. Projects is a hand-authored index kept in the repository:
  each entry gives its name, an image, an honest status including work that is
  still in progress, a candid description, and a link to where the project
  actually lives. It is not generated from an external service, and it is not a
  portfolio. Per-project detail routes remain a later editorial task.

The specification does not implement the production redesign. Final homepage
copy and portrait-note wording remain an editorial task within the approved
voice. Social-preview composition remains a separate design task. Owner
authentication, in-browser editing, draft storage, and Git-backed publishing
are separate product initiatives and must not compromise the public
publication's static, accessible, resilient core.
